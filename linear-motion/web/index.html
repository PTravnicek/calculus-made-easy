Linear Motion

A narrated, animated lesson on speed, velocity, acceleration and free fall, and how they show up as slopes and areas on a graph. The video stops to ask you questions. Below it are exercises, a motion lab and a ruler to catch.

After the video

Exercises

Ten problems on the ideas in the video. Type a number, a fraction such as 45/2, or a root such as sqrt(2). Use $g = 10$ m/s² unless a problem says otherwise, and take the direction of motion, or upwards, as positive.

Motion lab

Three graphs of one motion

Pick a motion, or set your own starting velocity and acceleration, and press Play. The distance graph's slope is the velocity, the velocity graph's slope is the acceleration, and the shaded area under the velocity graph is how far the body has gone.

Try it

Catch the ruler

Press Start, wait, and catch the ruler the moment it drops: press Space, click it, or tap it. How far it fell tells you your reaction time, through $t = \sqrt{2d/g}$.

The ruler hangs with its 0 cm mark between your fingers.

– cm fallen
– s reaction time

    Most people land between 15 and 25 cm, which is 0.17 to 0.23 s. A 30 cm ruler can only time reactions up to about 0.25 s.
